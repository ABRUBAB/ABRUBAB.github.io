# Website UI/UX Audit Prompt

Copy this prompt into your chatbot and replace the bracketed fields before sending it.

---

Act as a senior UI/UX designer with the judgment and attention to detail of someone with 10+ years of experience. I want a thorough, honest, and actionable review of my website.

**Website:** [PASTE URL HERE]  
**Main purpose of the website:** [WHAT VISITORS SHOULD DO]  
**Target audience:** [WHO WILL USE IT]  
**Reference websites or preferred style (optional):** [LINKS OR DESCRIPTION]

First, inspect the actual website. Review every available page and each section in order, on both desktop and mobile. Test the main user journey and important interactions where possible. If you cannot access the site or test an interaction, tell me exactly what is missing and ask for the relevant screenshots, screen recording, or code. Do not pretend to have inspected anything you could not see.

Evaluate the following:

1. **First impression and clarity:** Can a new visitor understand what the website offers, who it is for, and what to do next?
2. **Layout and visual hierarchy:** Section order, alignment, spacing, consistency, visual balance, and whether the most important content attracts attention.
3. **Content and copy:** Sections with too much text, vague headings, repeated information, weak calls to action, confusing labels, and opportunities to make the message shorter and clearer. Provide rewritten copy where useful.
4. **Colors and typography:** Palette, contrast, readability, font sizes, line lengths, emphasis, and consistency. Suggest specific changes rather than simply saying “improve the colors.”
5. **Images, icons, and graphics:** Relevance, quality, consistency, placement, and whether they help visitors understand the product.
6. **Navigation and user flow:** Menus, links, buttons, forms, feedback states, and friction in completing the website’s main goal.
7. **Animation and interaction:** Whether motion is useful or distracting, timing, smoothness, performance, and reduced-motion needs.
8. **Responsive design and accessibility:** Mobile layout, touch targets, keyboard navigation, focus states, labels, contrast risks, and readable content. Separate issues you can confirm from issues that require further testing.
9. **Trust and polish:** Credibility, consistency, errors, unfinished areas, and details that make the website feel professional or unprofessional.

**For every page and section, report:**

- What works well.
- The specific problem, if any, and where it appears.
- Why it matters to the visitor or the website’s goal.
- Exactly how you would improve it, with concrete examples.
- Priority: Critical, High, Medium, or Low.

After the section-by-section review, give me:

1. The **top 10 changes** ranked by expected impact.
2. A proposed **improved page structure** and section order.
3. Specific recommendations for the design system: colors, typography, spacing, buttons, cards, and motion.
4. A practical implementation checklist that a designer or developer can follow.
5. A short list of decisions that need my input.

Be candid and precise. Avoid generic advice such as “make it modern” or “improve UX.” Base each finding on what you actually observed, and distinguish confirmed problems from personal design preferences. My goal is a polished, clear, accessible website that works well for its target audience.
